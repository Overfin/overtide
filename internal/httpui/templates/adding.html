How adding works

Every hour, Overtide asks Overfin what to add: the titles your libraries lack, in the order Overfin gives them (its most valuable, with trending and popular titles mixed in by the discovery share set in Overfin). Requests go before them.

Every 15 minutes it tries the titles whose turn it is, one search at a time, at most 3 searches a run (12 an hour, a series' seasons each counting), so indexers aren't flooded. A title goes in Radarr or Sonarr once (their root folder and quality profile), unmonitored and tagged "overtide", and is never deleted from them. A try monitors it (for a series, the seasons Overfin suggests) and searches for it; if nothing started, it's unmonitored again and tried later: after 1 day, then 2, 4, 8, 16, then every 30. Two days before its release, a title is monitored without a search, so Radarr's or Sonarr's RSS catches the release. Once its download is confirmed, a title is unmonitored, so Radarr and Sonarr don't upgrade it on their own; if the download stops before its import, the title goes back in line.

At most 200 suggestions wait. One leaves after 5 tries that found nothing, or 60 days in the list. A title that left the libraries, or the list unfinished, isn't taken again for 6 months.

A disk's usage target is what Overtide steers toward: it adds first, and removal brings the disk back under the target. Only the critical threshold stops adding, until removal catches up.

Tags in Radarr and Sonarr say where each title Overtide manages came from: "overtide-added" (from Overfin's suggestions), "overtide-requested" (requested in Overfin), "overtide-found" (there before Overtide took it), "overtide-new" (added from outside while Overtide manages everything). Radarr and Sonarr don't record how a title was added: to tell your lists or other request apps apart, give them tags of their own there. Import lists are yours: Overtide doesn't read them.
